**Abstract**

Rainfall seasonality can promote insect activity in water-limited tropical dry forests or restrict it in humid lowlands, where soils waterlog and ephemeral resources decay faster. We asked which response occurs in a humid tropical landscape of the Middle Magdalena Valley, Colombia, and whether seasonal change is expressed through richness, dominance and species turnover or through the abundance of a few common species. We sampled dung beetles (Scarabaeinae) with baited pitfall traps at 39 sites in one rainy-season campaign (July 2021) and one dry-season campaign (March–April 2022) and recorded 38 species. Abundance per site was 54% lower in the rainy season, the direction expected if rain restricts activity. Richness did not differ significantly, including at equal numbers of individuals, and nearly all species occurred in both campaigns. Three dominant tunneler species accounted for most of the net loss of individuals, and without them abundance did not differ between seasons. Evenness was higher in the rainy season only for the pooled assemblage, guild responses differed weakly, and small and large beetles declined whereas medium-sized beetles did not. Rainfall at five IDEAM stations was similar during both campaigns, but the rainy-season campaign followed a much wetter month, and the campaigns were in different years, so the abundance contrast cannot be attributed to rainfall during sampling. Seasonal change thus operated mainly through the abundance of dominant species and not through species replacement. Monitoring should report abundance, dominance and functional groups, with sampling replicated across seasons and years.

**Keywords**: Scarabaeinae, beta diversity, functional diversity, humid tropical forest, seasonality.
